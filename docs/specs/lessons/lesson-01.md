# Lesson 1 draft: Assume the feeling of the wish fulfilled

Status: **draft, not approved**. It is blocked on the rights decision in #43 and on the review gates in `../lessons.md`. A dev sample only, never for production.

Source: Neville Goddard, *Five Lessons*, Lesson 1, "Consciousness Is the Only Reality" (Los Angeles, 1948), as transcribed at https://realneville.com/pdf/lesson1.pdf. The PDF is 10 pages, with no copyright notice and no transcriber credit. Page numbers below are pages of that PDF.

Quotes are copied from the PDF. Its few typing errors ("rnathematicians", "Tarmar") don't touch the quotes chosen. Where the PDF prints words in capitals for emphasis (HERE, NOW), the script uses normal case and keeps the words.

## Script (Calm mode, about 3 minutes)

N = narrator, in the listener's chosen voice (sample: HeyGen Nadine). Q = quote voice (sample: HeyGen Lucian).

| Who | Line |
|---|---|
| N | Lesson one. Assume the feeling of the wish fulfilled. |
| N | In 1948, Neville Goddard taught a small class in Los Angeles. Today we take three of his lines, one at a time, and see how each one can change the way you think today. |
| N | First, the idea at the centre of it all. Neville said: |
| Q | Man, by assuming the feeling of his wish fulfilled, alters his future in harmony with his assumption, for, assumptions though false, if sustained, will harden into fact. *(p. 3)* |
| N | Notice the word sustained. A passing hope is not an assumption. An assumption is a quiet decision to think from the end, as if it has already happened, and to keep thinking from there. |
| N | So today, when a worried thought arrives, you don't need to argue with it. Ask instead: how would I think about this if it were already done? Then think that way. |
| N | Second, where to put your attention. Neville said: |
| Q | You do not visualize yourself at a distance in point of space and at a distance in point of time being congratulated on your good fortune. Instead, you make elsewhere here and the future now. *(p. 9)* |
| N | Watching yourself on a distant screen keeps the wish far away, in some other place and some later time. Instead, choose one small moment that would follow the wish coming true, and step inside it. |
| N | Keep it to a single moment. A handshake. A message. A door opening. Feel it happening here, now, and play it again until it feels natural. |
| N | Third, how to let it settle. Neville said: |
| Q | A most effective way to embody a desire is to assume the feeling of the wish fulfilled and then, in a relaxed and drowsy state, repeat over and over again like a lullaby, any short phrase which implies fulfillment of your desire, such as, thank you, thank you, thank you. *(pp. 9–10)* |
| N | Thank you is the feeling of something already received. It is hard to be thankful and anxious at the same moment. |
| N | Your practice for today. |
| N | Tonight, as you drift toward sleep, choose one small moment that would follow your wish. Feel it here and now, and say softly, thank you. |

Quotes make up about a third of the words (Q is about 115 words of about 350). The explanation is the rest.
